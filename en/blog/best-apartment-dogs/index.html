Best dogs for apartment living: breeds that thrive without a yard

Updated July 4, 2026 · reading time approx. 6 min

Having a dog in an apartment without a yard is completely doable, as long as you pick the right breed. What decides it isn't just size, but temperament, exercise needs and the ability to settle calmly at home while you're at work.

What really makes a dog an apartment dog?

The common idea that “small dog = apartment dog” is only half the truth. A Chihuahua in an apartment? Fine. But a Jack Russell terrier, also small, will be thoroughly frustrated without a yard and an hour of exercise a day. The traits of a genuine apartment dog are:

  • Low to moderate exercise needs — two walks a day are enough for a contented life.
  • Calm behaviour indoors — no panicking, no chewing the furniture, no complaints from the neighbours.
  • Tolerance of being alone — ideally 4 to 6 hours without their owner around.
  • Reasonable barking — in a block of flats, consideration for the neighbours is a must.

Size matters mainly for space: in a studio flat you and a Bernese mountain dog will be squeezing past each other. But even a medium-sized dog with a calm temperament can feel great in an apartment as long as you walk them regularly.

Small breeds that thrive in an apartment

These small and toy breeds are practically made for apartment life:

  • French bulldog — quiet, friendly, undemanding on exercise. One of the most popular apartment dogs of all. Just watch out for heat and breathing problems.
  • Pug — a similar character to the French bulldog, easy-going and cuddly. Also a short muzzle, so they can't cope with hot weather.
  • Cavalier king charles spaniel — cuddly, calm and adaptable. A shorter walk satisfies them, but they long to be next to you all the time.
  • Bichon frisé — cheerful, practically non-shedding and very adaptable. Suitable for allergy sufferers too.
  • Maltese — an elegant miniature with a calm temperament. Needs coat care, but very little exercise.
  • Chihuahua — the smallest dog in the world, but beware: not suited to small children and often wary of strangers.

If you're looking for a dog for children in an apartment, read in more detail which breeds suit children — you'll find comparisons for family apartments there.

Surprisingly apartment-friendly large breeds

You might not believe it, but some large breeds are calmer indoors than many a terrier:

  • Greyhound or whippet — an athletic sprinter that spends most of the day on the sofa at home. After a morning warm-up in the park they rest for hours. Sensitive, quiet and a great companion.
  • Standard poodle — clever, non-shedding, exceptionally adaptable. Happy in an apartment as long as they get enough exercise and mental stimulation.
  • Cocker spaniel — medium-sized, affectionate and cuddly. Needs regular exercise, but doesn't require a yard.

As a rule: for a larger breed in an apartment you have to be willing to go out two to three times a day and give them at least an hour of active exercise.

What to prepare for in an apartment

An apartment without a yard puts more organisational demands on the owner. Pay attention to these points:

  • Regular outdoor exercise — without a yard, you are your dog's legs. Two outings a day aren't enough for more active breeds; for apartment types they usually are.
  • Lift or stairs? — for older or physically weaker dogs (especially short-legged or brachycephalic breeds), repeatedly climbing stairs is a strain.
  • The neighbour below you — young dogs jump and romp. A carpet muffles the noise, but good training eliminates it.
  • Agreement with the building — check the house rules or your lease; the property owner may restrict keeping a dog.

Summary: how to choose?

When choosing an apartment dog, watch three things: temperament (calm vs. active), daily exercise needs and the ability to tolerate being alone. The safest choice for a first apartment dog is the French bulldog, the cavalier king charles spaniel or the bichon frisé. If small breeds aren't for you, consider a greyhound or a cocker spaniel.
