Samoyed: Temperament, Coat, and Exercise Needs

Updated September 14, 2026 · breed guide

The Samoyed is a medium-to-large breed with Siberian roots, bred by indigenous peoples of northern Siberia to pull sleds and herd reindeer. It weighs roughly 17 to 30 kilograms and stands 46 to 60 centimeters at the shoulder, but what stands out most is its thick white coat and the trademark “smile” it’s famous for worldwide.

Temperament

The Samoyed is friendly, sociable, and almost always welcoming toward strangers, which makes it a poor guard dog but a wonderful family companion. It’s more vocal than many other breeds, happy to bark and howl, and without enough attention it can let you know it’s unhappy quite loudly.

Life with kids

★★★★★

Samoyeds generally do very well with children, they’re playful, patient, and rarely confrontational. Because of their strength and energy, playtime with small children is worth supervising, a Samoyed doesn’t always realize how boisterous it’s being.

Training and socialization

The Samoyed is intelligent but also independent-minded, historically used to work without constant direction, which shows up as a streak of stubbornness in training. It responds best to positive reinforcement and short, fun sessions, a heavy-handed approach tends to backfire. Without enough socialization and exercise, it’s prone to excessive barking and separation anxiety.

Exercise and energy

As a former sled and herding dog, the Samoyed needs at least an hour of active exercise a day, ideally with room to run. Without enough of an outlet it gets bored quickly, and boredom tends to show up as destructive chewing or excessive barking, this is not a couch-potato breed.

Health and care

The Samoyed’s thick double coat needs brushing several times a week, and daily during shedding season, which typically happens twice a year. The white color also takes more upkeep than it looks like it would, the coat shows dirt easily and tufts of fur turn up around the house year-round. The breed is prone to hip dysplasia, eye conditions, and diabetes, so regular vet checkups are worth it.

Who the Samoyed suits

The Samoyed suits active owners who have time for regular exercise and coat care and don’t mind a bit of noise. Thanks to its thick coat it handles cold climates better than hot ones, in summer it needs shade, water, and lighter exercise during the hottest hours.
